PART 14 · PRO

Complete Business Profit Calculation

Revenue is not profit. A ₹999 order does not mean ₹999 in your pocket. Product cost, fees, shipping, ads, returns and GST all have to be counted.

Selling price (customer pays)999
GST component (illustrative)−48
Manufacturing cost−400
Packaging−20
Gross profit531
Marketplace fees (illustrative)−200
Shipping (illustrative)−60
Advertising cost per order−100
Net contribution per order171

Example from Part 14. Fees, shipping and GST are assumptions in the book; replace each with your own actual numbers.

PART 11 · ADVANCED

Meta Ads: the metrics that matter

Meta Ads has no guaranteed returns. It is a paid testing channel. Start with a small budget, read the data, then scale.

  • CPM cost per 1,000 impressions
  • CTR clicks ÷ impressions
  • CPA spend ÷ purchases
  • ROAS revenue ÷ ad spend

Campaign naming example: SALES_OVRTEE_NOV26

PART 4 · INTERMEDIATE

Manufacturer red flags

Before you pay anyone, check for these warning signs in a manufacturer.

  • Only a WhatsApp number, with no address or GST
  • 100% advance payment and no written terms
  • A rate far below the market
  • Pressure to pay "today only"

Verify GST at services.gst.gov.in before any payment.

The ₹1 Crore Mathematics

Revenue, gross profit and net profit are three different numbers.

Important: ₹1 crore here is a mathematical business target, not a guaranteed income or result. Reaching it depends on the right product, cost control, low returns and continuous testing.

Revenue What the customer paid ₹999 order
Gross Profit Revenue minus product and packaging cost ₹531 (example)
Net Contribution Gross profit minus marketplace fees, shipping and ad cost ₹171 (example)
Net Profit Net contribution minus overheads, returns, RTO and tax Always lower than the contribution
₹27,397 Revenue needed per day for ₹1 crore a year (₹1 crore ÷ 365)
~27 Orders per day at ₹999 gross revenue (example)
~160 Orders per day at ₹171 net contribution, ignoring overheads (example)

These figures are simplified. They leave out GST, returns and overheads, and the book says so. Real profit will be lower. Use the per-order calculator in Part 14 with your own numbers before drawing any conclusion.
